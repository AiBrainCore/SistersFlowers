There is a quiet line that runs between highland mist and coastal light. On one end sits Da Lat — cool mornings, red soil, greenhouses breathing steam into the fog. On the other, Da Nang — salt air, bright afternoons, celebrations that stretch toward the sea. Sisters Flowers grew up on the first of those shores. Now we are preparing to open on the second.

This is not a franchise story. It is a studio story told in two cities.

## Why Da Lat came first

Da Lat taught us how flowers behave when the air is cool enough to keep petals calm. It taught us the rhythm of early markets, the patience of growers who cut stems at dawn, and the discipline of wrapping a bouquet so it still drinks water when it leaves our hands. Our highland studio at 04 Chi Lang is where the brand’s hands learned their craft: knife, ribbon, the day’s harvest, and the quiet confidence of arranging for people we would see again.

Customers already knew us as a local florist — not a catalogue of stock photos, but a place where flowers are prepared the way a neighborhood studio prepares them. That trust is the root we refuse to cut.

## What “two cities” means for Sisters

When we say Da Lat and Da Nang, we mean one language of care expressed in two climates. The same sisters’ eye for proportion. The same preference for stems that look alive, not lacquered. The same bilingual welcome — English and Vietnamese standing side by side — because guests, expats, and Vietnamese families all walk through our doors with stories that deserve clear listening.

Da Nang is preparing to open at 53 Luong Nhu Hoc, Hoa Cuong Bac, Hai Chau. Until the doors swing fully, Da Lat remains the beating workshop: sourcing, designing, and delivering with the calm of a highland morning. When Da Nang opens, it will not copy Da Lat like a stencil. It will carry the same standards into a coastal calendar — birthdays by the river, hotel lobbies that want quiet elegance, congratulations that need to arrive before sunset.

## How we keep one brand without flattening place

A network can become generic if every city gets the same plastic smile. We refuse that. City scope on our Journal and on our catalog exists for a reason: some stories belong to the plateau; some belong to the coast; many belong to both. Prices and availability can differ by city because logistics and local stem markets differ. What does not differ is the promise: flowers prepared with studio care, for moments that matter.

## The road between mist and sea

People ask whether expanding means we will leave Da Lat. No. Da Lat is not a chapter we close; it is the climate that formed our hands. Expanding means we can meet more of the people who already love this craft — families who split time between highland weekends and coastal workweeks, travelers who want flowers waiting at a hotel, friends who celebrate across provincial lines.

Two cities also means two kinds of light on the same stems. In Da Lat, roses hold their composure longer in cool air. In Da Nang, arrangements lean into brightness and movement — less fog, more open sky. Our designers learn both, so a Sisters bouquet still reads as Sisters whether it is carried through pine scent or past the Han River.

## What customers can expect as Da Nang prepares

Preparation looks unglamorous from the outside: supply conversations, studio layout, delivery routes, bilingual scripts for orders, the same ribbon discipline practiced until it is muscle memory. We would rather prepare thoroughly than open loudly. When Da Nang is ready, you will feel it in the details — stems that still drink, wrapping that protects, messages that sound like people, not templates.

Until then, order where we are open, follow the Journal for studio notes, and know that “coming soon” is not a marketing fog. It is a studio getting its knives sharp for a new morning.

## A shared invitation

Whether you are in the highlands or by the coast, flowers remain a language older than either city. Sisters Flowers exists to speak that language carefully. From Da Lat to Da Nang is not a slogan. It is the distance we are willing to walk so that the same care can arrive — mist or sea — when words need a bouquet beside them.
## Continuity for guests who move between cities

Many of our customers already live a two-city life: workweeks near the coast, weekends in the pines, family split across provinces. A Sisters account of care should feel continuous for them — same voice on the card, same seriousness about freshness, same willingness to hear a complicated brief. That continuity is the real product of expansion.

When Da Nang opens, you will still be able to start from the catalog, occasions, or custom path you already know. The city switcher will change logistics; it should not change trust.
